### 2. YOLO
- **What it is** · Ultralytics docs: detection finds and labels objects per frame; tracking "maintains a unique ID for each detected object as the video progresses"; output is detection output plus object IDs. Trackers: BoT-SORT ("extends ByteTrack with camera-motion compensation and optional ReID"), ByteTrack (lighter, static cameras), also OC-SORT, Deep OC-SORT, FastTracker, TrackTrack (default). · https://docs.ultralytics.com/modes/track/ · Gotcha: YOLO alone does not know pickleball. Rating/shot/"performance" logic is Dinkli's own layer on top. Copy should say "computer vision detects and tracks players and the ball; our analysis turns that into stats", not "YOLO rates you". Pose (keypoints) is a separate YOLO-pose model, not fetched (no primary data this run). Fixed cameras suit ByteTrack. · Confidence: H
- **Licensing** · Ultralytics license page lists Enterprise License as required for "any commercial product or service", SaaS/APIs using YOLO behind the scenes, embedded in cameras, and custom-trained models in commercial use; AGPL-3.0 compliance means releasing complete corresponding source of the whole derivative work. · https://www.ultralytics.com/license · Gotcha: HIGH. Dinkli is a commercial service; either buy Enterprise License or avoid Ultralytics code/weights (or open-source). Pitch-deck/diligence risk. Trademark/naming: the page has NO trademark statement; no data found on nominative-use rules for "YOLO" (name also used by non-Ultralytics models). Low-risk compromise: mention "computer vision (YOLO-family object detection)" in a technical section only, not as a brand claim; confirm with counsel. · Confidence: H (license page), L (trademark = no data)

### 3. Legal / privacy (not legal advice)
- **Texas CUBI, Bus. & Com. Code 503.001** · "Biometric identifier" = retina/iris scan, fingerprint, voiceprint, or record of hand or face geometry. Must inform AND get consent before capturing for a commercial purpose; no sale/disclosure except narrow exceptions; protect with reasonable care; destroy within reasonable time, no later than 1 year after the purpose expires; civil penalty up to $25,000 per violation, enforced by the AG. H.B. 149 (2025), effective 2026-01-01, added AI exemptions: training/processing/storing identifiers to develop/train/evaluate/offer AI systems is exempt "unless the system is used to uniquely identify a specific individual"; if later used for another commercial purpose, retention/destruction rules and penalties apply. · https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/BC/htm/BC.503.htm (fetch gave a summary with short quotes, not verbatim) · Gotcha: Body pose/silhouette is not listed, but FACE geometry is. Do not do face recognition/face-matching; identify players by account/jersey/booking position instead, and say so. If face/voice is captured, consent at booking is required. Audio: avoid voiceprints; consider recording video without audio. Confidence: H (statute), M (application to pose = interpretation; no case law found)
- **Texas AG enforcement** · Meta $1.4B settlement (first CUBI suit/settlement, filed Feb 2022); Google $1.375B settlement (finalized; included biometrics) · https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/news/releases/attorney-general-ken-paxton-secures-14-billion-settlement-meta-over-its-unauthorized-capture ; https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/news/releases/attorney-general-ken-paxton-secures-historic-1375-billion-settlement-google-related-texans-data · Gotcha: AG actively enforces; also has launched a facial-data investigation of Meta glasses (listed on AG site). Confidence: H
- **Not researched (no data this run; flag for counsel):** COPPA (under-13; practical rule: require 18+ or guardian consent for minors, no recording of under-13s without verifiable parental consent), bystanders/other courts, audio consent (Texas is one-party consent for participants, but a camera crew recording others' conversations is a different case; safest is no audio), Texas TDPSA, Texas HB 149 TRAIGA. 
- **Privacy policy/FAQ should say (recommendation):** what is recorded (video, no audio; no face recognition), purpose (analysis, stats, rating), consent captured at booking/check-in with on-court signage, opt-out option (player not recorded or blurred; no penalty), who sees the video (the player, opt-in sharing only), retention period (set a firm number, e.g. delete raw footage on a stated schedule, well under 1 year), deletion request path (existing delete-account page), no sale of footage, minors policy.
